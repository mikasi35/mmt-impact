## Why this programme exists

Localisation is agreed in principle across the sector and slow in practice. The
reason is rarely a lack of willing local organisations. It is that direct funding
carries compliance requirements — financial controls, procurement documentation,
safeguarding systems, audit readiness, monitoring frameworks — which many capable
organisations have never had the resources to build.

The gap is a systems gap, and it is closable. That is the work of this programme.

## What an assignment looks like

We start with an honest capacity assessment against the standard the organisation
actually needs to meet, not a generic checklist. That produces a development plan
with a sequence, because attempting everything at once produces documents nobody
uses.

Then we work alongside the team implementing it. A financial procedures manual
delivered as a PDF changes nothing. The same manual, developed with the finance
officer who will operate it and tested through a real reporting cycle, changes the
organisation.

## Monitoring, evaluation and research

We design results frameworks that can actually be measured with the data an
organisation can realistically collect, and we conduct baselines, assessments,
evaluations and research to that standard.

Where an evaluation finds that a programme did not achieve what it set out to
achieve, that is the finding we report. An evaluation function that only produces
favourable findings has no value to anyone, including the organisation
commissioning it.

## Where this capability comes from

Institutional strengthening work draws on MMT Impact’s own operating experience and,
where an engagement calls for it, on expertise available across the wider MMT
Group in procurement, supply chain, finance and regulated service delivery. Any
such arrangement is set out explicitly in the relevant proposal — MMT Impact does
not present another organisation’s track record as its own.
