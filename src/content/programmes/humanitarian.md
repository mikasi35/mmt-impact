## Why this programme exists

Humanitarian response is judged on speed, coverage and principle. Resilience work
is judged on whether the next shock does less damage than the last. Communities
facing recurrent crisis need both, and separating them into different programmes
run by different organisations on different funding cycles is a structural
weakness in how the sector operates.

MMT Impact runs response and resilience as a continuum. The same assessment that
identifies who needs assistance today identifies why this community is exposed, and
that finding informs what we design next.

## Principles and coordination

We work to humanitarian principles — humanity, neutrality, impartiality and
independence — and we respond within the established coordination architecture
rather than alongside it. That means participating in cluster coordination, sharing
assessment data, accepting tasking, and not deploying into an area already covered
because it is more visible.

## Preparedness

Preparedness is unglamorous and consistently underfunded. It is also the point at
which the cost per life protected is lowest. Where funding permits, we work with
communities on early warning, contingency planning, protection of productive
assets and pre-positioned response arrangements — before the season that will test
them.

## Accountability during response

Speed is not a reason to suspend accountability. Feedback mechanisms, safeguarding
obligations and complaint handling apply during an emergency response exactly as
they apply in development programming, and we report on them the same way.
